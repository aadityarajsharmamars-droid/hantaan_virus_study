Divergent evolution of cap-snatching endonucleases: Influenza PA (6FS6) vs Andes virus L N-terminus (6Q99)

Summary
Evidence from sequence, taxonomy, structure/chemistry, pharmacophore geometry, and cross-target docking indicates that the influenza PA endonuclease and the Andes orthohantavirus L endonuclease NTD are homologous domains that have diverged while conserving a core catalytic pharmacophore. This supports divergent evolution rather than convergence.

Taxonomy: shared ancestry and point of divergence
- Shared lineage: Viruses → Riboviria (realm) → Orthornavirae → Negarnaviricota → Polyploviricotina
- Divergence:
  • Influenza A (6FS6): Insthoviricetes → Articulavirales → Orthomyxoviridae (Alphainfluenzavirus)
  • Andes orthohantavirus (6Q99): Bunyaviricetes → Elliovirales → Hantaviridae (Orthohantavirus)
Source: reports/taxonomy_mapping.csv

Sequence relatedness (PDB-derived chains)
- Global alignment aligned length ≈ 191 aa with ≈ 33.5% identity.
  • This level over ~200 aa is consistent with homology (outside the deep twilight zone).
  • FASTAs prepared for robust testing with HHsearch: analysis/sequences/6FS6_chainE.fasta, 6Q99_chainA.fasta

Structural classification and fold context
- 6FS6 maps in CATH to 3.40.91.90 (Alpha–Beta; 3-layer (aba) sandwich; “Restriction Endonuclease” topology; Influenza PA endonuclease domain).
- 6Q99 CATH mapping was not returned by the PDBe endpoint at query time. Literature classifies hantavirus L endonuclease NTD as a homologous cap‑snatching endonuclease fold.

Pharmacophore geometry (6 Å radius around centers)
- Conserved kernel: 2× hydrogen-bond acceptors (ACC) + 1× hydrophobe (HPH), shared by both pockets
- Local kernel alignment RMSD: 1.849 Å (pharmacophore_compare/triangle_rmsd.txt)
- Type composition overlap score: 0.30 (multiplicity-aware Jaccard; pharmacophore_compare/summary.txt)
- Divergent features:
  • Andes: multiple donor (DON) and positive (POS) hotspots
  • Influenza: negative (carboxylate) site and metal coordination (MET) when using a larger radius

Cross-target docking comparison (AceDock; 248 ligands)
- N common ligands: 248
- Medians: 6FS6 = -28.77; 6Q99 = -21.89; Δ (ANDV − PA) = +6.84 (more negative is better)
- Wilcoxon signed-rank p = 5.71e-41 (significant difference)
- Dual-top-quartile ligands saved in analysis/dual_top_quartile.csv
Files: analysis/summary.csv, analysis/merged_scores.csv

Interpretation: evidence for divergent evolution
- The coherent sequence signal (≈33.5% identity over ~191 aa) and the shared catalytic pharmacophore strongly indicate a common ancestral endonuclease domain, with functional constraints maintaining the 2×ACC + 1×HPH geometry.
- Divergence is evident in surrounding pocket features and metal usage (influenza PA shows robust Mn coordination in the co-crystal; Andes L NTD did not show metals under the prepared structure conditions). These chemical/architectural differences explain the significant shift in docking score distributions.
- Taxonomy shows a shared high-rank ancestry (Orthornavirae → Negarnaviricota → Polyploviricotina), with divergence at the class/order/family level (Orthomyxoviridae vs Hantaviridae), consistent with domain-level divergence across lineages that retained cap‑snatching function.

Conclusion
- Combined taxonomy, sequence, structural/chemical mapping, pharmacophore alignment, and docking statistics support homologous (divergent) evolution of the influenza PA and hantavirus L endonuclease NTDs.
